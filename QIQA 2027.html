QIQA

Quantum-Inspired and Quantum Algorithms for Real-World Applications

A half-day workshop on algorithms that solve real problems on classical and GPU hardware today, and on the quantum algorithms that will take them onto quantum hardware. We ask the same question of every application: what runs well now, what will run on quantum devices, and how do we tell the difference?

About

Why this workshop

Quantum-inspired methods such as tensor networks, classically simulated quantum walks and Ising-machine heuristics already deliver value on classical and GPU hardware. They are also the natural stepping stone toward their quantum counterparts, and the fairest baseline to measure those counterparts against.

QIQA brings together algorithm designers and practitioners from finance, networked systems, life sciences and logistics. Talks cover the full path from a problem formulation to GPU simulation to quantum hardware, with results reported against both classical and quantum-inspired baselines.

The workshop is organized jointly by Chung Yuan Christian University (Taiwan) and Keio University (Japan), together with the NVIDIA quantum team in Taiwan. We plan QIQA as a recurring Taiwan–Japan series co-located with QCNC. Accepted papers, slides and a short post-workshop report will be archived on this site.

Call for papers

Topics of interest

We invite original, unpublished work on quantum and quantum-inspired algorithms applied to real problems. Topics include, but are not limited to:

Quantum-inspired methods

Tensor networks (MPS, TTN, PEPS), classically simulated quantum walks, simulated bifurcation and other Ising-machine heuristics, dequantized and randomized linear-algebra methods.

Quantum algorithms for applications

QAOA and its variants, constrained QUBO/Ising formulations, quantum annealing, quantum sampling and distribution loading, amplitude estimation and quantum Monte Carlo.

Quantum and quantum-inspired machine learning

Variational quantum circuits and quantum kernels, quantum generative models, tensor-network models, and hybrid quantum–classical training, applied to application tasks and compared against classical baselines.

Graphs and networks

Quantum walks for graph analytics, anomaly and community detection on transaction and communication networks, network design and routing as optimization.

Application domains

Portfolio optimization, risk, market simulation, fraud and AML detection, drug discovery and chemistry, recommendation, logistics and scheduling, energy, semiconductor manufacturing.

From simulation to hardware

GPU-accelerated simulation workflows, resource estimation, hardware experiments and error mitigation, benchmarking, reproducibility studies and negative results.

Reporting checklist

Papers that report an application result should state four things, so results can be compared across talks.

  • Classical baselineThe best classical method the result is compared against.
  • Quantum-inspired baselineA tensor-network, quantum-walk, Ising-machine or similar method run on classical hardware.
  • PlatformGPU simulation, emulator or quantum hardware, with the device or simulator named.
  • Compute budgetWall-clock time, hardware used, and shots or samples where relevant.

Authors

Submission guidelines

Originality

Original work that has not been published and is not under review at another conference or journal.

Format

Papers must be written in English and submitted as PDF, using the standard IEEE templates for Microsoft Word or LaTeX. IEEE templates

Length

Up to 6 printed pages. The main text, including figures, tables, appendices and any material other than references, must not exceed 6 pages; references do not count toward the limit.

Papers that do not comply with the length limit will not be reviewed.

Review

Single-blind. Every submission receives at least two reviews from the technical program committee, and the program co-chairs make the final decisions.

Criteria

Relevance to the workshop theme, technical soundness, novelty, clarity, reproducibility, and comparison against both classical and quantum-inspired baselines.

Conflicts

No reviewer is assigned from an author's institution or from among their co-authors of the past three years.

Publication

Accepted and presented papers will be published in the IEEE QCNC 2027 Conference Proceedings, together with main-track papers, and submitted to IEEE Xplore. There are no separate workshop proceedings.

Code and data artifacts are encouraged.
